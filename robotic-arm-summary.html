4 D.O.F Hand Gesture Controlled Robotic Arm

Second-Year Mechanical Engineering Project (2024 - 2025)

This project presents the design and fabrication of a 4 Degrees of Freedom (DOF) robotic arm aimed at providing a low-cost, functional platform for educational and research purposes. Inspired by industrial robotic systems, this prototype replicates basic human arm movements—rotation, lifting, and placing—using servo motors (MG996R and SG90), 3D-printed PLA components, and Arduino-based control. The current prototype offers four core motions: base rotation (yaw), shoulder movement (pitch), elbow movement (pitch), and wrist rotation (roll). It is controlled through hand gesture recognition using computer vision techniques implemented with OpenCV, providing an intuitive and interactive user experience.

This prototype serves as a foundational model, developed to understand feasibility and functionality, with a comprehensive version to follow after further study. Future iterations aim to incorporate additional degrees of freedom, enabling greater flexibility and application potential. Such enhancements could allow the arm to perform tasks like object sorting, assistance for individuals with disabilities, automated pick-and-place operations, and collaborative tasks in educational and small-scale industrial settings. The project emphasizes modularity, simplicity, and innovation, making it a valuable learning tool in robotics and mechatronics.
